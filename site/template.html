Can two chart indicators beat simply holding NVDA?

I took two popular TradingView indicators, rewrote them in Python, combined them into exact buy and sell rules, and replayed those rules on 14 years of real NVIDIA prices. Then I compared the result with the simplest alternative: buying once and never selling.

In plain English

  • What it isA program that decides, day by day, whether to own NVIDIA stock or hold cash. A backtest replays past prices and records what would have happened if the rules had been followed exactly.
  • The benchmarkBuy & hold: buy once in January 2013 and never sell. Any trading system has to beat this to be worth the effort.
  • The resultThe system turned $1 into __S_MULT__. Holding NVIDIA turned $1 into __B_MULT__. The system lost to buy & hold, which is the normal outcome for a stock that rose this much.
  • What it did wellIt stepped aside in bad markets. In 2022 NVIDIA fell 50% while the system made 0%, and its worst fall from a peak was __S_DD__ against __B_DD__ for buy & hold.
  • What it showsTurning a visual idea into precise rules, testing it fairly (trading costs, no use of future prices, automated tests), and reporting the result honestly, including when it is not good.

How the system decides

Signals are checked at the end of each trading day and the trade happens at the next morning's opening price, so the system never acts on information it could not have had.

1 · Trend filter · Smart Money Concepts

Only trade when the trend is up

The price has broken above its last major high, so the bigger trend is up. If the trend is down, the system stays in cash.

2 · Entry · WaveTrend

Buy the dip inside that uptrend

A momentum indicator turns back up after a pullback. That suggests the dip is ending, so the system buys.

3 · Exit · whichever comes first

Take profit, follow the trend, or cut the loss

Sell when momentum looks stretched, when the big trend turns down, or when price falls below the last short-term low (a stop loss).

How I made sure the test is fair

  • No peeking at the future. An automated test cuts the price history off at five different dates and checks that not a single past signal changes. If the code used future prices, it would fail.
  • Realistic costs. Every buy and sell pays 0.10% for fees and slippage.
  • Every trade checked by hand. All __TRADES__ trades were re-calculated from the raw prices and matched to the cent.
  • No tuning to the data. Both indicators use their standard default settings, so the result isn't fitted to NVIDIA's past.
  • Each part tested alone. Each indicator was also run on its own, to see what each one adds.

What I learned

  1. Timing a strong winner is very hard. NVIDIA rose about 580 times. The system was invested only __INMKT__ of the time, and the missed days cost more than the avoided crashes saved.
  2. The trend filter works as a safety brake, not a profit engine. It sat out the whole 2022 crash, but it also missed most of the 2023 rally.
  3. Winning trades were bigger than losing ones. About __WIN__ of trades made money, yet the system still grew, because the average win was much larger than the average loss.
  4. Stop losses can't stop overnight gaps. In November 2018 NVIDIA opened about 19% lower after earnings, and that single trade lost 23%.
  5. Next steps: test on many stocks rather than one famous winner, size positions by risk instead of all-in or all-out, and avoid buying right before earnings.
